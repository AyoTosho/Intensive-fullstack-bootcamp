Annie Wilson on Film Cameras,
Saying No to Clients & Why
Slow Work Still Wins

Annie Wilson spent a decade shooting campaigns for fashion labels before walking away to run a to-person studio from a converted garage in Brunswick. Her new series, Stil Life, was shot enirely on medium format film over three years. We sat down with her to talk about work and process.

Every project starts the same way, a week with no camera at all. Wilson walks the location, sits it on meetings,and writes notes nots ina pocket notebook. Only once she can describe the final image in a single sentence does she pick up a camra. Her process comes down to three rules she repeats to every client:

  1. Shoot less, look longer, print everything.
  2. Never show a client picture you wouldn't hang on your own wall. Edit before you share.
  3. leave room for the accidents that make a series feel real.

Wilson's next project is quiter still. She's spending the winter photograping a singlr orchard in the Yarra Valley, one flame a day,for a book she hops to publish in 2027. There's no client and no deadline.

"Everyone asks what the business model is "she laughs." Honestly, I'm not sure there is one.But every time I've followed the slower idea, the work has been better and the right clients have followed. I'm happy to make the bet."

What's next

In an industry that reward speed and volume, Annie Wilson has built a pratice of the doing the opposite: fewer clients, fewer frames and more time spent looking. it isn't a formula she sell, and she'd be the first to say it took a decade of fast wor to earn the right to slow down.

What she offers instead is a reminder that creative career doesn't have to scale to be successsful. Her studio is two people, one camera and awaitlist. That's a delibrate choice, and one she revisits every year.

Still Life opens at Northside Gallery in Fitzroy on 14 November and still run until the end of January. A limite-edition book of the series, designed by Theo Marsh, is available to pre-order her website.

As we leave, the rain has stopped. Wilson is already outside with the Mamiya, waiting for the light to come back.
